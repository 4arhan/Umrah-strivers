Hajj and Umrah ask a lot of you at once: papers, rulings, crowds, heat, and a head full of duas. Miqah holds the practical side, so your attention can stay where it belongs.

01

Before you fly

Ready before the airport.

Tell Miqah whether you are going for Umrah or Hajj and when you leave. It works out the rest.

Home

A countdown that knows what’s next

Give Miqah your departure date and Home becomes your plan: days to go, how prepared you are, and the next thing to sort out, with anything overdue flagged.

  • Umrah or Hajj
  • Tamattu’, Qiran or Ifrad
  • Apply your group’s setup from one link

Plan › Prepare

One checklist for papers, health, packing and the heart

Passport, visa, vaccines, bookings and packing, each with when to do it and why. It begins with the intention. Rules that change, like visas and permits, show the date they were last checked.

  • Share your readiness
  • A list for children who travel
  • Documents vault with a PIN

Plan › Prepare

Your days, city by city

A suggested itinerary for the length of your trip: Makkah first or Madinah first, at a standard or a family pace. Tap a day to change it.

  • The days of Hajj built in
  • Share it with your group

Plan › Learn and Quiz

Learn it before you live it

Short lessons, a daily deck of knowledge cards, and a quiz that unlocks level by level once you score 80%. Each answer shows its source.

  • Separate levels for Hajj
  • Kids’ corner and stories
  • A certificate at the end
02

In ihram

The rites, one step at a time.

When you are tired and the crowd is moving, you should not have to remember what comes next.

Umrah › Steps & duas

Every step in order, with its dua

The whole rite as a walkthrough: what to do, what to say and why. Duas come in Arabic, transliteration and meaning, with a Listen button where your phone has an Arabic voice.

  • Guidance for sisters
  • A story for the kids
  • Report a correction on any step

Umrah › Counters

Count rounds without losing your place

One big ring for tawaf and one for sa’i. Tap after each round and Miqah tells you which round you are on, with the dua for where you are standing.

  • Wudu check before tawaf
  • Undo a round
  • Nafl tawaf too

Focus mode

Eyes on the Kaaba, not the screen

Go full-screen and tap anywhere to count. The screen stays awake, and on phones with a built-in voice the count can be read aloud through your earphones.

  • Tap anywhere
  • Screen stays awake
  • Spoken count, off by default

More › Duas

The duas you were asked to make

Keep a list of your own duas and the ones people asked of you. Read them full-screen, one at a time, and mark each as asked.

03

The days of Hajj

It knows which day of Hajj it is.

From the 8th of Dhul Hijjah, the whole app turns to face the day you are in.

Home

The day’s rite and its window

During the days of Hajj, Home shows the rite for the day and when it must happen. On 9 Dhul Hijjah that is the standing at Arafah, from Dhuhr until sunset.

  • Dates you can correct
  • Your camp on every card

Daily › Today

A checklist for the day you are in

Each day of Hajj has its own list, from the intention and the Talbiyah on the 8th to the last day of stoning, beside your prayer times and your camp.

Hajj › Counters

Seven pebbles, counted per pillar

A counter for each jamrah on each day, with the time window for stoning that day.

  • Stoning on someone’s behalf
  • If a pebble misses
  • Log it later if you could not tap

Hajj › Counters

The Jamarat, drawn simply

A sketch of the three pillars in the order you meet them, and where to pause for dua. It is not to scale: your group gives you the route, the level and the time.

Home

A plan for the heat

The signs of heat exhaustion and heatstroke, what to do, and who to call: 911, 997 for the Saudi Red Crescent, and 937 for the Ministry of Health.

04

Every day there

Make every day count.

The days between the rites are the ones that slip away. Miqah gives each of them a shape.

Daily › Today

The day at a glance

Prayer times for Makkah and Madinah, the day’s worship checklist, your hotel and meeting point, and a row for each child’s day.

  • Tick a prayer in one tap
  • Reminders if you want them
  • Times worked out on the phone when offline

Home

Ramadan mode

Travelling in Ramadan? Home counts down to iftar and suhoor, and marks the odd nights of the last ten.

Places

Ziyarah with purpose

The sites of Makkah and Madinah, each with what happened there, a practical tip, a one-tap map, and a card to show your driver in Arabic.

  • Near me
  • Walking times from your hotel
  • Mark places visited

Places

What you need before you go

Where a visit needs a permit, like the Rawdah, Miqah says so, links the official page, and shows the date it last checked.

Daily › Today

One line a day

A private journal with one line for each day. It stays on your phone and in your backup, and one line can sit on your keepsake image.

Home

Home again

After you return, Miqah turns to keeping the habits: prayers on time, Quran and dhikr for thirty days, and a start on your next journey.

Safety and family

When words fail, show the screen.

Six cards made to be handed to a stranger. Arabic first, your language underneath, and always white so they can be read in full sun. Print them as pocket cards before you fly.

Trust

Every reference numbered. Every changing fact dated.

A wrong ruling does real harm, so Miqah shows its working. Facts that change, like visas, permits, vaccines, phone numbers and transport, carry the date they were last checked and a link to the official page.

Sources

Every hadith and Quran reference in one searchable list, with its number, its grade where one is stated, and where the app uses it.

Report a correction

A button under every article, dua, step and place opens an email with the item already named. Miqah sends nothing itself.

What’s new

After each update, a short note says what changed in plain words.

A study companion, not a fatwa service. Citations have been checked against the source texts. Review by a qualified scholar and a native-speaker check of the Arabic phrase cards are still pending, and the app says so. For a ruling on your own situation, ask a scholar you trust.

Private by design

No account. No ads. No analytics.

Miqah has no server to send your details to.

Nothing to sign up for

What you enter is saved in your phone’s own storage. The developer cannot see it.

Works with no signal

Once opened, Miqah runs offline. With a connection it fetches only prayer times, its fonts and its own updates.

Yours to keep or erase

Export a backup file whenever you like, restore it on a new phone, or reset and wipe everything.
